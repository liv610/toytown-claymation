Railroad of Hope · concept for a Toys for Tots-related project · Prototype

Making the map, Toy Town and the finder feel like one world

Two directions each for the mobile landing, the Toy Town story interface and the transitions between them, then one recommendation. These are static concepts drawn over real renders of the current felt map and the existing Toy Town stills. The working Milestone 1 app was not changed. Campaigns are fictional test data, the story text is the existing draft copy, and links are placeholders.

Recommendation

Map-first ticket on the landing, a cinematic story with the storybook’s step railroad, and one camera for arrivals and the finale

  1. Mobile landing: Direction A, the ticket. The map fills the phone. One paper ticket docks at the bottom: the ZIP field on top, Explore Toy Town below a perforation. Borrow B’s glowing Toy Town junction so the map itself points to the story. It is the fastest route to a campaign website and the smallest change from what works today.
  2. Story: Direction A, cinematic, on desktop and phones. The scene is the whole screen and the words sit on it like subtitles. The one visible extra control is “Find your local campaign”. Take the storybook’s four-station railroad as the Chapter 3 step tracker, because it makes the four steps read as one short journey.
  3. Transitions: Concept 1, one continuous world. Explore Toy Town flies down with the existing descent. The finder rises as the same paper ticket over the paused scene. The finale rises back out to the national map, with the ZIP field right under “Find yours.” With reduced motion, every flight becomes Concept 2’s quiet crossfade.

Why this combination: the miniature world stays full-screen at every step, the interface becomes a small set of paper objects that belong to that world (a ticket, a tag, a sign), and the finder looks and behaves the same in all three places. The storybook direction is lovely on desktop but shrinks the scene on phones, which is the opposite of what the review asked for.

Exploration A · highest priority

Mobile landing

Both at 390 × 844 with no scrolling, plus a 375 × 667 small phone. The current landing stacks a header, a paragraph, the map, the Explore card and the finder. Both directions cut that to the map plus two clear doors.

Direction A · Map-first ticket

Recommended

What’s different

No panels at all on the map: one physical ticket, docked where thumbs are. Search and story share it, separated by a perforation, so they read as two halves of one decision.

Improves

The map gets about 75% of the screen instead of about 30%. Both journeys sit inside thumb reach, and the result keeps the website button in the same place the Find button was.

Tradeoffs

The ticket covers the southern edge of the map. The intro sentence and map credits move behind (i), so the map has to explain itself through its labels.

Effort

Low to medium. It restyles today’s #landing. The map already frames a campaign above a bottom-docked finder (freeArea). The keyboard state needs visual-viewport handling and real-device testing.

Direction B · Search header, Toy Town on stage

What’s different

A composed page in two zones: a search header, as on any site people already know, and a map that is a stage aimed at Toy Town, with an in-world thread to its button.

Improves

Both journeys are understood at a glance, and the map points at the story instead of being a backdrop. The search field stays visible above the keyboard.

Tradeoffs

Search sits at the top, out of thumb reach. The closer map shows less of the country. A paper header is more “website” than A, and the hanging tag covers the middle of the map on a result.

Effort

Medium. It needs a new header search, a new phone framing of the national view (a closer camera) and the hanging-tag layout. Framing under a top panel already exists.

Exploration B

Toy Town story interface

Both keep every Milestone 1 control (four chapters, progress, Back, Next, Find, Exit, Skip, Read as text) but give them different weight. Chapter 3’s four steps become four beats in both, using the existing draft text unchanged. The numbered signs are drawn over existing stills for this concept.

Direction A · Cinematic

Recommended

What’s different

No story card. A thin top row and subtitle text over a soft shadow at the foot of the scene. Controls are ranked: Next is red, Find is outlined gold, and Skip and Read as text are quiet.

Improves

The scene goes from about half of the phone screen to all of it. Chapter 3 drops from one long list to four short beats. It is also the layout a full-screen 3D stage needs later.

Tradeoffs

Captions must stay short (two lines on phones), and text over moving 3D needs the shadow scrim. Skip and Read as text sit one tap deeper on phones.

Effort

Medium. Restyle story.js’s DOM and add a small menu. Chapter 3 becomes four beats in story_data.js (the same words). The signs are an overlay on the still stage, with navigation, state and finder hand-off unchanged.

Direction B · Storybook

What’s different

An editorial layout: the scene is framed like an illustration, the words are typeset like a book page, chapters are tabs and navigation is turning pages.

Improves

The most readable option, with large type and no text over the scene. Read as text is almost the same view. On desktop it feels crafted and calm.

Tradeoffs

On phones the scene shrinks to a plate, so the world is less the focus than in A. A book is a second metaphor next to the railroad and the map, and it risks feeling like a slideshow with nicer paper.

Effort

Medium. A responsive spread layout, tabs and page-corner controls. The stage adapter fits the plate unchanged, and a 3D scene would render into a smaller frame.

Exploration C

Transitions between the map, Toy Town and the finder

Static storyboards at desktop size. The flight frames are real frames of the existing descent (__nat.shot), which today lands in Maple Hollow’s street. The gold glow and signs are drawn over for the concept. Nothing here was animated or built.

Concept 1 · One continuous world

Recommended

What’s different

No screen changes. The visitor never leaves the felt world: down into the town, back up to the country. The finder is one paper ticket wherever it appears.

Improves

It turns the story into a place the visitor visits. The finale connects “one town” to “your town” through geography, which is the project’s idea.

Tradeoffs

The flights cost a few seconds, so they must be skippable and instant under reduced motion. On weak phones the flight may stutter; that is unmeasured, since the cloud has no GPU.

Effort

Medium to high. The descent and makeFlight exist, and a reverse flight is new work. The descent ends in Maple Hollow, not in the felt Toy Town, so it needs the “one Toy Town” decision. Without it the flight can end in a crossfade to the Toy Town still.

Concept 2 · Storybook pages

1 · Map to Toy Town: the map softens and a book opens

→ →

2 · Toy Town to the finder: the facing page turns

3 · The finale: a fold-out map

What’s different

Cuts instead of flights. The map stays where it was behind the book, and every change is a page.

Improves

Calm, quick and predictable. It works the same on every device and with reduced motion, and the title page sets expectations.

Tradeoffs

It is less magical: the map and the town feel like two artefacts, not one world. The fold-out finale is a picture of the map, not the map.

Effort

Low to medium. Only the DOM and CSS change, with no camera work. It is also Concept 1’s reduced-motion fallback, so building it first is not wasted.

Built into every concept

The small UX decisions

One Find Local Campaign per context

The desktop top bar drops its Find Local Campaign button while the finder ticket is on screen (see the landing frames). In the story, “Find your local campaign” appears once, in the top row, plus the in-place action on Chapter 3, step 1 and the finale.

Back to results only when there are results

One serving campaign shows its card with only “Change”. A back link appears only when two or more campaigns serve the ZIP. Nearby campaigns that don’t serve the ZIP are never listed as equals: they would appear under their own “Not in your area” heading only when nothing serves it.

The website stays the clearest action

After every search, in every concept, Visit Campaign Website is the one large red button, in the same place as the Find button before it.

Labels and provisional links

Campaigns carry the Test data tag, and placeholder links say example.org. Make a Monetary Donation is marked Provisional until an official destination is confirmed. Every scene is labelled a placeholder.

Before implementation

Decisions still open

  • Which Toy Town the camera lands in. The existing descent ends in Maple Hollow’s street inside the national app. The felt Toy Town in the stills lives in the separate journey prototype. Concept 1 needs one destination; until then the flight can end in a crossfade to the still.
  • Chapter 3 as four beats. The words stay exactly as drafted, but the chapter grows from two beats to five (the opening line plus four steps). Please confirm that structure, and the felt signs as the visual cue (in 3D later they would be props on the existing street, not a new environment).
  • Moving the map caption and credits behind (i). The Census attribution stays one tap away. Is that acceptable for the landing?
  • The finale’s ZIP field. I recommend putting the finder’s field directly under “Find yours.” instead of a button that opens it. It removes a step, and the result appears as the same ticket.
  • Request Toys on phones. In “Ways to help” it should open the finder directly with request-specific wording, never the story. Wording for that request path needs your and the organisation’s review.
  • Story copy and accuracy. Unchanged draft. It still needs the organisation’s review before publication.
